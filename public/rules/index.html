I

Quick Start

Play your first battle in about an hour, using only troops, a single scenario and the core loop. Add Knights, magic and gear once the basics feel familiar.

What you need

  • 9–12 miniatures per player on 25–32mm round bases, plus 40mm bases for characters later.
  • A handful of six-sided dice (D6), a tape measure in inches, and a 48" × 48" table with a few pieces of terrain.
  • A few tokens or a spare die per model to track lost limbs and lives.

Your first battle in four steps

  1. Take a starter warband

    Each player uses one of the two starter musters below (about 140 points each). No characters, no spells, no gear yet.

  2. Set up a Field Battle

    Place 4 pieces of terrain. Roll off; the winner picks a table edge. Take turns placing one model at a time within 8" of your own edge.

  3. Play 4 rounds

    Each round: roll for priority, then take turns activating one model at a time in the Move, Shoot and Fight phases. The next page shows the loop.

  4. Count kill points

    Score 1 kill point every time you slay an enemy model, including models that come back from Nirvana and fall again. Most kill points wins.

The core loop

Phase 1Priority

Both roll a D6. High roll activates first this round.

Phase 2Move

Take turns moving one model up to its M. Touching an enemy is a charge.

Phase 3Shoot

Take turns shooting with models that aren't in combat and didn't charge.

Phase 4Fight

Resolve each combat. Chargers strike first, then survivors strike back.

Phase 5End

Slain models with lives left will return next Move phase.

Every attack in one breath

Roll a D6 for each Attack on the weapon and hit on its Hit value. Roll to wound by comparing weapon Strength to target Toughness. The target saves on its armor, worsened by the weapon's AP. Each wound that gets through takes a limb (a D6: 1–3 arm, 4–6 leg), unless the wound roll was a natural 6 or the weapon has Damage 2, in which case it is Lethal and the troop falls at once. Most troops have 2 W, so a second limb hit also fells them.

When you're ready for more

Add one Paragon to each side (Chapter XIII), then magic (Chapter X), then Knights (Chapter XII) and the full 500-point muster (Chapter III). Roll on the scenario table (Chapter XIV) once you've played a Field Battle or two.

II

The Basics

Models, profiles, dice and measuring: the conventions every other chapter relies on.

Models and warbands

Each player commands a warband. Its rank and file are troops, drawn from the twelve Amtgard classes. They are led by characters: Paragons, the masters of a single class, and Knights, the highest honors of the realm. Every character in this game is a named player from the Kingdom of Westmarch.

Mount troops on 25–32mm round bases and characters on 40mm bases. Distances are always measured between the closest points of two bases.

The profile

Every model has six characteristics. How many attacks it makes, what it needs to hit and how hard it strikes all come from its weapon, not from its profile.

Dice

  • D6 is a six-sided die. D3 is a D6 halved, rounding up. 2D6 is two dice added together. Scenarios are chosen with a D8.
  • A value written as 4+ means the roll must equal or beat 4.
  • Re-rolls: a die can be re-rolled only once. Re-roll before applying modifiers.
  • Natural rolls: a natural roll is what the die shows before modifiers. A natural 6 always hits and a natural 1 always misses.
  • Roll-offs: both players roll a D6 and the higher roll wins. Re-roll ties.

Measuring

Players may measure any distance at any time. Moves, ranges and auras are all measured in inches from base edge to base edge. A model is within a distance if any part of its base is.

From the field

Everything in this book borrows from real Amtgard play: limbs before lives, the walk to Nirvana, class abilities, Knights of the five orders and the battlegames fought every week in the parks of Westmarch.

III

Mustering a Warband

Before every battle, each player musters a warband and writes it on a roster. Then both players run the pre-battle sequence together.

The muster

  1. Agree the size

    A standard game is 500 points per side. For a shorter game, play 300. Up to 30% of your points (150 in a standard game) may be spent on characters, including their upgrades.

  2. Choose your characters

    Pick named Knights and Paragons from Chapters XII and XIII. You may take each Knight order only once, and each named person only once.

  3. Equip your characters

    Choose each character's weapon and armor (free for characters), then spend their upgrade budget on field abilities and gear: up to 25 points for a Paragon, 50 for a Knight. Multi-belted Knights may buy abilities from their other orders here.

  4. Muster your troops

    Spend the rest of your points on troops (Chapter IX). Pay for each troop's weapon and armor, and give any troop one Potion if you like.

  5. Fill the spellbooks

    Every Healer, Druid and Wizard buys spells: 3 Spell Points for troops, 6 for Paragons. Spellbooks cost no army points.

  6. Name your General

    Choose one model to lead. A troop General lasts longest (6 lives); a Knight General hits hardest (4 lives). Losing the General for good tests the whole warband's nerve.

  7. Write the roster

    Record each model, its loadout, its points and its lives. Show your opponent the finished roster before the pre-battle sequence.

The pre-battle sequence

  1. Choose a scenario

    Roll a D8 on the scenario table (Chapter XIV), or agree on one.

  2. Set the field

    Take turns placing 4–6 pieces of terrain, each more than 3" from the others, unless the scenario says otherwise.

  3. Pick edges

    Roll off. The winner chooses a table edge; the other player takes the opposite edge.

  4. Deploy

    Starting with the roll-off winner, take turns placing one model at a time within 8" of your own edge. Models held in reserve (such as Ambush) are set aside.

  5. Before the first round

    Resolve pre-game abilities: Forward Scout moves, Masterwork upgrades and anything else marked "before the game". Then begin round 1.

IV

The Battle Round

A game lasts 6 rounds. Players take turns activating one model at a time inside each phase, so neither army acts all at once.

Phase 1Priority

Roll a D6 each. High roll has priority.

Phase 2Move

Alternate moves, charges and fall-backs.

Phase 3Shoot

Alternate shooting and spellcasting.

Phase 4Fight

Alternate choosing combats to resolve.

Phase 5End

Score, test morale, tidy effects.

Priority

Both players roll a D6. The higher roll has priority for the round and activates first in every phase. On a tie, priority passes to the player who did not have it last round. In round 1, roll off.

Activations

  • In the Move, Shoot and Fight phases, players alternate, starting with the priority player. Each activation is one model taking its action for that phase.
  • When one player has no models left to activate, the other activates the rest of theirs one after another.
  • "Its next activation" means the model's next action in any phase.

Heroic Actions

At the start of the Move, Shoot or Fight phase, a character may declare a Heroic Action. That character and up to 3 friendly troops within 3" act first in that phase, before the priority player. Knights have 2 Heroic Actions per game and Paragons have 1. If both players declare one in the same phase, roll off to see whose heroes go first.

The Move phase

  • Moving: a model may move up to its M in any direction. It can't move through other models or within 1" of an enemy unless it is charging.
  • Charging: a model that can see an enemy within its M may move into base contact with it. It has charged this round. A model with a lost leg can't charge, and no model can charge across an obstacle.
  • Engaged: a model in base contact with an enemy is engaged. Engaged models may only stay where they are or fall back.
  • Falling back: an engaged model may move up to its M directly away from every enemy it touches. It can't shoot, cast or charge for the rest of the round.
  • Returning models: models coming back from Nirvana are placed at the start of the phase, before the first activation (Chapter VI).

The Shoot phase

  • Each model that is not engaged and did not charge or fall back may make one ranged attack or cast one spell.
  • The target must be within the weapon's range and visible to the shooter (Chapter VII). Engaged enemies can't be targeted by ranged attacks.
  • Casting follows the magic rules in Chapter X.

The Fight phase

  • A combat is a group of engaged models touching one another. The priority player picks the first combat to resolve, then players alternate.
  • Inside a combat, models that charged this round strike first. Then the surviving models of the other side strike back. If no one charged, the priority player's models strike first.
  • Each model attacks with its melee weapon. Split its Attacks among enemies it touches, or among enemies within 2" if its weapon has Reach, before rolling.
  • A model slain before it strikes does not strike.

The End phase

Score Victory Points as the scenario says, take any morale check caused this round (Chapter VI), and end effects that last "until the end of the round".

V

Attacks and Wounds

Ranged and melee attacks use the same five steps. A wound takes a limb before it takes a life.

Making an attack

  1. Roll to hit

    Roll one D6 per Attack on the weapon. Each roll that equals or beats the weapon's Hit value is a hit.

  2. Roll to wound

    For each hit, compare the weapon's Strength with the target's Toughness on the wound chart. Set aside any natural 6s.

  3. Armor save

    The target rolls its Sv for each wound, worsened by the weapon's AP. A roll of 1 always fails.

  4. Ward save

    If the armor save fails, a model with a ward rolls it. AP never affects wards.

  5. Limb or Lethal

    Each wound that gets through is a limb hit or a Lethal hit.

Hit modifiers

A natural 6 always hits and a natural 1 always misses. All penalties to hit together (cover, a failed morale check, Cloak of Shadows, Suppression Arrow and so on) can never total worse than -2.

Limb and Lethal hits

As in Amtgard, most blows take an arm or a leg. Only a natural 6 to wound, or a heavy weapon, strikes the body.

ResultWhenEffect
Limb hitAny wound that is not LethalLose 1 W, then roll a D6: 1–3 arm, 4–6 leg
Arm lostLimb roll of 1–3No shield and no two-handed weapons (Two-Handed Long, Great Weapon, Spear, Bow). Other weapons get -1 A (minimum 1). With no usable weapon, fight as a Single Short with A1
Leg lostLimb roll of 4–6M becomes 2" and the model can't charge
Lethal hitNatural 6 to wound, or any wound from a D2+ weaponA troop is slain outright. A character loses W equal to the weapon's D + 1
  • A hit to a limb that is already lost costs the W but has no extra effect.
  • Some rules make wound rolls of 5+ or 4+ Lethal. These never stack: use the best one.
  • A model is slain when its W reaches 0 and goes to Nirvana (Chapter VI).
  • Healing: each W restored also restores one lost limb, chosen by the healing player.
  • Mortal wounds skip armor and ward saves and count as limb hits, unless the rule calls them Lethal.
Example

A Barbarian with a Great Weapon (A2, Hit 4+, S5, AP -2, D2) strikes a Warrior in heavy armor with a shield (4+ save). It rolls a 4 and a 2: one hit. S5 against T3 wounds on 3+, and it rolls a 3. The Warrior's save becomes 6+ after AP -2 and it rolls a 5. The wound gets through, and because the Great Weapon has D2 it is Lethal. The Warrior goes to Nirvana.

VI

Lives, Generals and Morale

Every model has several lives. The slain walk to Nirvana and return to the fight, until they run out.

ModelRevivesLives in total
Knight34
Paragon45
Troop56

Nirvana and revives

  • Nirvana: a slain model is removed from the table and set aside in its warband's Nirvana. Mark where it fell.
  • Returning: at the start of the next Move phase, each model in Nirvana with revives left spends one and returns within 3" of its own table edge with full W and all limbs. It may move that round but can't shoot or cast.
  • Out of lives: a model slain with no revives left is out of the game.
  • Resurrection: Resurrect, the Resurrection Scroll, Rekindle and similar effects target a friendly model slain this round. It returns where it fell, or within 1" of the model using the effect, and does not spend a revive.
  • No revives: summoned or converted models (Summon Beast, Undead Minion) have none.
  • Models in Nirvana don't count for Presence, Leadership or auras.
  • Tracking: keep a die beside each character, and tally troop lives on the roster. Each datasheet in Chapter XII has a lives track you can tap.

The General

During the muster, each player names one model as their General. It may be a Knight, a Paragon or a troop. A General uses the normal revives for its rank.

Morale

  • Army morale check: when the General is out of lives, the warband takes a morale check in that round's End phase. Roll 2D6 against the highest Leadership among your models still on the table. The check passes if the roll is equal to or under that Leadership.
  • Failed check: every model in the warband gets -1 to hit for the rest of the game. This counts toward the -2 limit on hit penalties.
  • Leadership tests: every other Leadership test works the same way: roll 2D6 and pass on a roll equal to or under the model's Ld.
VII

Terrain and Cover

Shooting into cover is -1 to hit. Cover never affects melee.

Line of sight

Check from the model's own point of view. A shooter must be able to see at least part of the target. Impassable terrain blocks line of sight completely.

Cover

  • In cover: a target is in cover if it is inside area terrain, within 1" behind an obstacle that lies between it and the shooter, or partly hidden by terrain or other models.
  • Effect: ranged attacks against a target in cover get -1 to hit. Spellballs get -1 to the cast roll instead.
  • High ground: a shooter on a hill ignores cover from obstacles, but not from area terrain.

Weapon rules

  • Reach 2": the model may strike enemies up to 2" away as well as those in base contact.
  • Shieldbreaker: the target gets no shield bonus to its save.
  • Wrap: a flail's chain wraps around shields, so the target gets no shield bonus.

Armor Damage

When a model wearing body armor fails an armor save, its armor becomes Damaged: worsen its save by 1 for the rest of the game (heavy 5+ becomes 6+; light 6+ becomes no save). Shields are never Damaged. The Warrior's Restore Armor ability, the Mend spell and an Armorer's Kit restore Damaged armor.

X

Magic

Every caster uses one system: buy a spellbook before the battle, then roll one die to cast.

Healers, Druids and Wizards, and their Paragons, are Spellcasters, each with its own list.

  1. Spellbook

    During the muster, a troop caster spends 3 Spell Points (SP) and a Paragon caster spends 6. Each spell bought gives one use per game; buy a spell again for more uses.

  2. Casting

    In its Shoot activation, instead of attacking, a caster may cast one spell at a target in range and line of sight. Touch means within 1".

  3. Cast roll

    Roll a D6. Equal or beat the Cast value and the spell works, spending the use. Fail and nothing happens, but the use is kept. A natural 1 is a fizzle: nothing happens and the use is spent.

  4. Spellballs

    A successful cast counts as a hit. Roll to wound and save as normal with the spell's profile.

  5. Engaged casters

    An engaged caster may cast only Touch or Self spells, in its Fight activation instead of attacking.

XI

Gear

Weapons, Armor, Relics and Potions. Characters buy gear from their upgrade budget; any troop may carry one Potion.

  • A model may take each piece of gear only once.
  • Relics are unique: one of each per warband.
  • Weapon gear upgrades the model's existing weapon. Armor gear stacks with the armor it already wears.
  • Each Potion is used once, in the drinker's activation, on itself or a friendly model within 1".
XII

Knights of Westmarch

Knights are the champions of the realm: 75 points each, with an upgrade budget of 50. Every Knight on the field is a named Knight of Westmarch.

Heroic

Knights and Paragons are Heroic: +1 Attack with any weapon, the weapon's Hit value improves by 1 (best 2+), and they take weapons and armor at no cost.

The five orders

Named Knights

  • Fielded order: a named Knight uses the profile and abilities of the order they are listed under.
  • Unique: each named Knight may appear once per warband, and the one-Knight-per-order limit still applies.
  • Multi-belted: for +10 points per other order they hold, a Knight gains one ability from each of those orders. Sir Monkey, listed under Sword and belted in Crown, Flame and Serpent, could cost up to 105 points.
  • Field abilities: a Knight buys field abilities from the list of any class they are a Paragon of. A Knight with no Paragonship picks one class list.

The roster holds 30 Knights. The core 17 are the four most senior Knights of each order by belting date (Westmarch has one Knight of Battle). Eleven more join because they also hold Paragonships, Dame Wendy the Wench rides with the Serpent, and Sir Kismet, the Privateer Caballero Augustus Rodriguez, the Navigator, joins the Flame as a guest Knight of note from the Kingdom of the Nine Blades. Each is listed under their earliest knighthood. Source: ORK Knights list.

Datasheets

Each Knight has a datasheet with a 3:4 frame for their player template graphic. Until that art is ready, the frame shows the portrait or heraldry from the Knight's ORK profile. Tap a shield on the lives track to mark a life spent; the marks stay in this browser only.

Signature abilities

Every named Knight can write a personal ability with us through the signature form on this site. A finished signature appears on that Knight's datasheet and is part of their profile at no extra cost. Optional extras written with a signature are bought from the Knight's 50-point budget. So far: Sir Rose Thorn's Pinning Arrow and Gravekeeper Sir Spade's Edge of Reach.

XIV

Scenarios

Eight battlegames, most of them fought in Amtgard parks every week. Roll a D8 or agree on one. The player with more Victory Points after round 6 wins.

Common rules

  • Table: 48" × 48" with 4–6 pieces of terrain, unless the scenario says otherwise.
  • Deployment: within 8" of your own table edge (shaded on each map).
  • Objectives and Presence: 40mm markers. You control a marker if you have more Presence within 3" of it. Troops count as 1, Paragons 2, Knights 3. Frozen models and models in Nirvana count as 0.
  • Kill points: 1 per enemy troop slain, 3 per Paragon and 5 per Knight, every time a model is slain. Kill points break ties in every scenario.
  • Revives: standard in every scenario. In Unlimited scenarios, troops never run out; characters keep their normal count.

A

Playtest Notes

These are the rules most likely to need tuning. Check them first at the table.

  • Character limit vs. upgrades: a fully equipped Knight costs 125, leaving 25 of the 150-point limit, which is not enough for a Paragon. Consider raising the limit to 40% (200 points).
  • Knight cost: 75 points is about five troops for a champion-level model. If Knights dominate, tighten the character limit before raising their cost.
  • Knight of the Sword: Heroic plus Bladesong gives 6 attacks with Dual Short, hitting on 3+ and Lethal on 5+ to wound.
  • Battle Knight aura: with about 22 models on the table, a 6" aura reaches many of them. Fallbacks: a 3" radius, or a limit of 3 models.
  • Warband size: about 22 models per side is closer to Warcry or Mordheim than Kill Team. Play 300 points for a smaller game.
  • Lethal thresholds: damage boosts are written as "wound rolls of 5+ are Lethal" rather than +1 D, since +1 D on a D1 weapon would make every wound Lethal. Watch whether 5+ Lethal on Barbarians is still too strong.
  • Lives and game length: with 4–6 lives per model, few warbands will be wiped out in 6 rounds. Objectives and kill points decide most games.
  • Named characters: the Knights and Paragons are real Westmarch players. Get their blessing before sharing the game widely.

Still to write

  • Signature abilities: 2 of 30 written (Sir Rose Thorn, Gravekeeper Sir Spade); the rest are collecting on the site
  • A castle or keep siege scenario, with gates, walls and towers
